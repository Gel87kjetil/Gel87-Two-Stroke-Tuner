Combined intake tract and resonance estimate Piston-port + reed branches

Geometry: measure along the flow centerline. The common tract runs from the carb exit to the branch point. From there, enter separate paths to the piston port and reed cage. Resonance timing and flow quality are reported separately.
Venturi/fuel estimate: use measured orifice diameters, not only stamped jet numbers. The model compares air opening area with the main-jet plus needle-annulus restriction and pilot circuit. It is useful for relative changes, but it cannot calculate a trustworthy AFR because PWK emulsion tube, air bleeds, fuel head, pressure pulsation and discharge coefficients are not fully known.
Simple explanation of the resonance regions

Acoustic fundamental is the natural quarter-wave frequency of the tract itself. The more useful motor comparison is the engine-order coincidence: the same tract frequency may line up with every intake pulse, every third pulse-pattern component, or every fifth. Therefore a tract with a 24,000 RPM-equivalent fundamental can show an interesting 3rd-order region near 8,000 RPM and a 5th-order region near 4,800 RPM. These are possible reinforcement regions, not guaranteed power peaks.

Piston branch also shows an open-period travel estimate using the actual piston-port duration. Reed branch is shown separately because reed stiffness, opening and crankcase coupling can shift the real response considerably.

Crankcase volume, compression and transfer ΔP Theoretical / measured comparison

Dynamic model: crankcase compression starts only after effective intake closure. Before closure the intake/reed/rotary path can exchange mass with the intake system, so geometric TDC-to-BDC compression overstates pressure. The model compares geometric and dynamic crankcase compression.
Measurement guidance

Preferred: assemble and seal the crankcase, position the piston at TDC, vent the highest point and fill from a calibrated burette with a compatible low-viscosity non-volatile calibration fluid. Do not use gasoline. Measure channel volumes separately by sealing both ends and filling from a burette, or export sealed volumes from CAD. Record trapped hoses, fixtures and dead volumes.
